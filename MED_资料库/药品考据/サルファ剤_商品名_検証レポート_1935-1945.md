# 日本におけるサルファ剤（スルホンアミド剤）商品名・製造会社 検証レポート 1935–1945

Verification report on claimed Japanese sulfonamide trade names, 1935–1945.
All statements are labelled **検証済み / VERIFIED**, **部分検証 / PARTIAL**, or **查不到 / UNVERIFIED**.
Nothing below is inferred or invented; where evidence is absent it is stated as absent.

---

## 0. 方法上の注意（Method / access limitations）

* `web_fetch` **cannot read PDFs at all** (`unsupported content type "application/pdf"`) and is
  **blocked** for `*.blogspot.com`, `collections.nmth.gov.tw`, `web.archive.org`, `kotobank.jp` (403).
* **Workaround that worked**: PowerShell / Python `urllib` **can** reach the network in this sandbox, so
  prewar journal PDFs were downloaded and their embedded OCR text layers extracted with `pypdf`.
  This produced the two strongest primary sources below (1941 and 1942 official test reports).
* Caveat on OCR: the 1941/1942 reports are scanned prewar typesetting with noisy OCR. **Drug names,
  dates, journal titles and Latin/German terms are reliable; surrounding kanji wording is approximate.**
  Quotations below are given as extracted, with obvious OCR garbage flagged.

## 2. The single most important source: 厚生省東京衛生試験所彙報 第59號 (1942)

**Citation:** 寺田（正中）「スルホンアミド剤の毒性に關する調査報告」, 厚生省東京衛生試験所彙報
第59號 (昭和17年/1942), pp. 83–84.
**PDF:** http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/059(1942).pdf (149 pp., OCR text layer present)

This 1942 official report contains a **list of the Japanese trade names actually being sold in Japan**.
Extracted verbatim (OCR noise in brackets):

> 「此ヂセプタールAこそ有名なるウリ・ロン（ウレロン）にして**日本にてはアルバジール・
> ウリノーゲン・ユーロン・ヂセプタール・ネオアドロンとして販賣さる**。叉ヂセプタールBは
> 外國名ネオウリロン，**日本名アナビオン**と稱さる。」

> 「**我藥局方名はスルフアミン**，米國にてはスルフアニールアミドと云はれ一般に此名を以て通稱とさる。」

> 「…外國名プロンチリン・ストレプトチツド・白色プロントジール・リソコツチン・プロンタルビン・
> **日本名テラポール・ゲリゾン・ルジール・白色アクチヅール・ポンヂール・プロセプチン・プレニン**等に
> して…」

> 「一九三七年に至りては米國の Rosenthal がヂスルフアニールアミド…の合成を發表す。前者は
> ヂズロンとして販賣せられ**日本名をアルバジールC**と稱す。」

> 「叉一九三八年には英國の Whitby が有名なるスルフアピリヂン…を發表し，M&B 693，或は
> デヂナンとして販賣し，ドイツ國にてはオィバジンとして販賣す。**我國に於ては全然獨自の研究に
> 依りて，津田・鈴木兩氏が昭和十四年之が合成並研究を發表し，現在アヂプロン及びトリアノンと
> して販賣さる。**」

> (on Albucid) 「…本品はスルフオンアミド剤中最優秀品と云はるるものにして，**日本にては
> ネオポレオン・レギオンとして販賣さる**。」

> 「更に一九三九年に至りて津田鈴木兩氏がスルフアメチールチアゾールの効力を報告し，
> **ネォヂセプタールとして販賣す**。」

> 「其他本邦に於ては**ホノカジール又はナルロン**と稱する四基のスルフオンアミド剤，及び
> **ネオポンジール**と稱するヘキソーゼとスルフアニールアミドとの結合體が市販に出づ。」

**Caveat:** the report gives **Japanese trade names but not manufacturers**. Manufacturer attributions
in this document come from *other* sources (§3–§5).

### 2b. Companion source: 同彙報 第56號 (1941) 「市販スルファミンの試験成績（第1報）」

**PDF:** http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/056(1941).pdf

States that in 昭和10年 (1935) Prontosil's specific efficacy was confirmed and became a world sensation,
and that p-aminobenzenesulfonamide alone was, **from 昭和14年 (1939)**, listed in the 日本薬局方 under the
name **スルファミン**, with 7 commercial products ("市販品7種") tested. It complains that products of
poor quality were exploiting the pharmacopoeia name "スルフアミン" — i.e. mass domestic production
under that name by 1940–41.

### 2c. 同彙報 第60號 (1943)

Lists the revised pharmacopoeia names **スルフアミン／ヂスルフアミン／アセトスルフアミン** and
tests スルファミン, ヂスルファミン, アセトスルファミン, スルファピリヂン, スルファメチルチアゾール.
Confirms ジスルファミン (disulfanilamide) and アセチルスルファミン as standard wartime Japanese articles.

## 3. Drug-by-drug findings

### 1) プロントジル / プロントジル (Prontosil) — **部分検証 / PARTIAL**

* **(a) Exact Japanese name:** **プロントジル**. Contemporary Japanese sources distinguish
  **赤色プロントジル** (Prontosil red, water-insoluble) and **水溶性プロントジル** (Prontosil soluble).
  The spelling **プロントジル** (with ジ) is the form used in the 1942 official report; **プロントシル**
  also appears in chemical-dictionary lists as a variant romanisation.
* **(b) Company:** **Bayer / I.G. Farben** (Germany). Synthesised Nov 1932, patented Dec 1932 (kept secret),
  patent published Dec 1934, Domagk's paper Feb 1935.
* **(c) Sale in Japan:** **Imported, not made under licence.** The American business-history study states
  plainly that **"product patents were not yet recognized in Japan"**, so Japanese firms simply made
  their own process-modified copies rather than taking licences — i.e. the Japanese Prontosil-era drugs
  were **unlicensed domestic copies**, not licensed production.
  * Earliest Japanese clinical report using Prontosil: **Feb 1936**, 林謙敏・荒木鵄弭郎「可能性疾患に
    対するプロントジルの応用」東京医事新誌 2968号 p.455 (a Dec 1935 Kumamoto Imperial University
    surgical-round case series using **Prontosil samples supplied by the maker** — 「プロントジルの試供品」).
  * By 1938 Bayer-made Prontosil was in routine Japanese hospital use (a 1938 Japanese paediatrics
    article discusses deaths during 「プロントジル」治療).
  * The 1941 official report describes German-produced Prontosil (プロントジル) as having 特効,
    i.e. it was a known imported article.
* **(d) Sources:**
  * 厚生省東京衛生試験所彙報 第59號 (1942) — http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/059(1942).pdf
  * 同 第56號 (1941) — http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/056(1941).pdf
  * Yongue, *Japanese Research in Business History* 22 (2005) 109–135 —
    https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jrbh2004/22/0/22_0_109/_article/-char/ja
  * Japanese clinical-use chronology (blog, 千駄木菜園, cached locally as `_pdf_fetch/blog1_15.txt`,
    `blog6_76.txt`) — https://tkobays.blogspot.com/2017/07/blog-post_15.html and
    https://tkobays.blogspot.com/2017/08/blog-post_76.html
    *(note: blogspot.com is not fetchable from this sandbox; text was retrieved by a sibling agent)*
* **NOT verified:** the specific **Bayer/IG-Farben Japan sales agent or importing house**, and the exact
  first date Prontosil went on sale in Japan. **查不到**.

### 2) スルファミン (Sulfamine = p-aminobenzenesulfonamide) — **検証済み / VERIFIED**

* **(a) Exact name:** **スルファミン** — this is the **official 日本薬局方 (Japanese Pharmacopoeia) name**,
  not a trade name. The 1942 report says literally 「我藥局方名はスルフアミン」.
  Related official articles: **ジスルファミン / ヂスルファミン**, **アセチルスルファミン /
  アセトスルファミン**.
* **(b) Companies — Japanese domestic production:** From 昭和14年 (1939) onward, **domestic mass
  production**; the 1941 official report tested **7 different commercial products** all sold under the
  name スルファミン and complained that poor-quality goods were exploiting the name. Specific firms
  documented for the *sulfanilamide* product itself:
  * **第一製薬 (Daiichi Seiyaku)** — **テラポール / Therapol**, launched **July 1937**, its own company
    history calls it **「国産第一号サルファ剤」 / "its first domestically produced sulfa drug"**.
    Derivatives: 二基テラポール (1939), テラポール軟膏 (1940), ネオ・テラポール (1941).
  * **山之内製薬 (Yamanouchi Pharmaceutical)** — **ゲリゾン / Gerison**, **July 1937**; based on the
    **French (Pasteur Institute) sulfanilamide** finding.
    In **1939**, `Gerison` (ゲリゾン) appears in the Japanese chemical-substance dictionary as a
    **synonym of sulfanilamide** — independent confirmation that it was a sulfanilamide brand.
  * **塩野義製薬 (Shionogi)** — **アクチゾール** (1937), **白色アクチゾール** (1938),
    **アクチワイス** injection (1939); アクチゾール was Shionogi's **No.1 selling product in FY1940**.
  * Other Japanese brand names of sulfanilamide-type products listed in the 1942 report:
    **ルジール、白色アクチヅール、ポンヂール、プロセプチン、プレニン、アナビオン** (makers 查不到).
* **(c) Year on sale:** **1937** (first domestic products, July 1937); official pharmacopoeia name from
  **昭和14年 / 1939**.
* **(d) Sources:**
  * Daiichi Sankyo official corporate history —
    https://www.daiichisankyo.co.jp/about_us/mission-strength/history/daiichi/
    ("1937 国産第一号サルファ剤となった細菌性疾患薬テラポール®を発売")
  * Daiichi Sankyo "Our Stories" —
    https://www.daiichisankyo.com/our_stories/detail/index_4519.html
    ("Daiichi had already launched its first domestically produced sulfa drug, Therapol, in 1937")
  * 厚生省東京衛生試験所彙報 第56號 (1941), 第59號 (1942), 第60號 (1943) —
    http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/056(1941).pdf ·
    http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/059(1942).pdf ·
    http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/060(1943).pdf
  * 日化辞Web / Weblio chemical dictionary — `Gerison`/`ゲリソン` listed as a synonym of
    sulfanilamide — https://www.weblio.jp/content/Bactecid
    (canonical JST record: https://nikkajiweb.jst.go.jp/nakkaji_web/pages/top.jsp?CONTENT=syosai&SN=J4.829A)
  * 山之内製薬50年史 (1975), table of contents shows chapter
    **「ゲリゾン」と「アルバジル」の開発** —

### 3) スルファピリジン (Sulfapyridine) — **部分検証 / PARTIAL**

* **(a) Exact Japanese trade names:** **アヂプロン** and **トリアノン** (both forms spelled with ヂ in
  prewar text; modern transliteration アジプロン). The 1942 official report: 「現在アヂプロン及び
  トリアノンとして販賣さる」. Foreign names in Japan: Dagenan / M&B 693 / デヂナン / オィバジン.
* **(b) Company:** **塩野義製薬 (Shionogi)** — **アヂプロン (sulfapyridine), 昭和14年 (1939)**.
  Shionogi's centenary history (「シオノギ100年」, 1978) lists 昭和14年: アクチワイス（注射剤）、
  ウリノーゲン（二基スルフォン剤）、**アヂプロン（スルファピリジン）**.
  **トリアノン's manufacturer: 查不到 / UNVERIFIED.**
* **(c) Year on sale:** **昭和14年 / 1939**. Independently corroborated: 津田・鈴木 published the
  **domestic synthesis and research in 昭和14年 (1939)**, wholly original Japanese work
  (「我國に於ては全然獨自の研究に依りて」).
* **(d) Sources:**
  * 厚生省東京衛生試験所彙報 第59號 (1942) — http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/059(1942).pdf

### 4) スルファチアゾール (Sulfathiazole) — **部分検証 / PARTIAL**

* **(a) Exact Japanese trade name:** **サルゾール** — verified as the **domestic** Japanese
  sulfathiazole product, explicitly contrasted with the US-made articles in a peer-reviewed title:
  「米製『サルファダイアジン』『サルファチアゾール』及び**国産『サルフアチァゾール』（サルゾール）**
  の男子淋疾に対する治驗」.
* **(b) Company: 查不到 / UNVERIFIED.** No manufacturer for サルゾール could be documented.
  (Related domestic product: スルファメチルチアゾール sold as **ネオヂセプタール**, per the 1942 report;
  manufacturer also 查不到.)
* **(c) Year on sale:** The cited article is **1952**, which only proves サルゾール was on the Japanese
  market by 1952. Sulfathiazole was synthesised internationally in **1939**, and the 1942 official report
  already discusses スルファチアゾール and スルファメチルチアゾール as articles under test in Japan,
  so a domestic product existed **by 1941–42** — but the **exact launch year of サルゾール: 查不到**.
* **(d) Sources:**
  * 藤原聞一ほか「米製『サルファダイアジン』『サルファチアゾール』及び国産『サルフアチァゾール』
    （サルゾール）の男子淋疾に対する治驗」岡山医学会雑誌 64巻5supplement号 (1952) pp.214–221 —
    https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/joma1947/64/5supplement/64_5supplement_214/_article/-char/ja
  * 厚生省東京衛生試験所彙報 第59號 (1942) / 第60號 (1943)

### 5) スルファダイアジン / スルファジアジン (Sulfadiazine) — **検証済み、ただし戦後**

* **(a) Exact Japanese trade name:** **テラジアジン (Theradiazine)**.
* **(b) Company:** **第一製薬 (Daiichi Pharmaceutical)**, manufactured at the Yanagishima plant.
* **(c) Year on sale:** **1948** — **outside the 1935–1945 window.** Daiichi's own history states the
  company "launched **Theradiazine**, a sulfadiazine … in 1948 … marketed as **Japan's first domestic
  pharmaceutical product**" (i.e. first domestically perfected new drug), with fewer side effects than
  conventional sulfadiazine. Sulfadiazine itself was synthesised in **1940** (American Cyanamid).
  *Related:* **1947 sulfaguanidine** (スルファグアニジン) also launched by Daiichi for dysentery/enteritis.
  *Pre-war analogue:* the 1942 official report lists the Japanese name of **ヂズロン (Disulon)** as
  **アルバジールC** — a different compound, not sulfadiazine.
* **NOT found:** any Japanese wartime (1935–45) trade name for sulfadiazine. **查不到**.
* **(d) Source:** https://www.daiichisankyo.com/our_stories/detail/index_4519.html
  (and the Japanese timeline https://www.daiichisankyo.co.jp/about_us/mission-strength/history/daiichi/)

### 6) アルブシッド (Albucid) — **your premise needs correcting — 部分検証**

* **The active ingredient is NOT what you were assuming.** Albucid is
  **sulfacetamide = p-aminobenzenesulfonacetyl-anilide (スルファセタミド)**, published by
  **M. Dohrn u. P. Diedrich** (Germany, 1938) and described in Japanese sources as
  「スルフオンアミド剤中**最優秀品**」. It is **not** sulfanilamide and **not** the same substance as
  Yamanouchi's Albasil.
* **(a) Exact Japanese names:** **アルブシッド** is treated as the **foreign name**.
  The **Japanese trade names** are given as **ネオポレオン** and **レギオン**
  (1942: 「日本にてはネオポレオン・レギオンとして販賣さる」). Contemporary Japanese medical papers
  also use the transliteration **アルブチッド** (e.g. 「Ullrichはアルブチツド使用の際…」,
  「岩下氏等は毎日…アルブチツド…」), i.e. **アルブシッド／アルブシド／アルブチッド** all occur.
* **(b) Company:** German origin (Dohrn/Diedrich). **Japanese manufacturer of ネオポレオン / レギオン:
  查不到 / UNVERIFIED.**
* **(c) Year:** c. **1938–1942** in Japan (documented as on sale in the 1942 official report).
* **On アルバジル / アルブシル:** **アルバジル is a real, well-documented Japanese trade name, but it is
  NOT Albucid.** It is **Yamanouchi's domestic version of Uleron** (see §7). **アルブシル** as a Japanese
  trade name: **查不到 / UNVERIFIED** — no source found. Do not conflate アルブシッド (Albucid) with
  アルバジル (Albasil); they are different drugs and different companies.
* **(d) Sources:** 厚生省東京衛生試験所彙報 第59號 (1942) —
  http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/059(1942).pdf ;
  同 第56號 (1941) (mentions 「市販品名 Albucid」 の淋疾治療成績, and p-Aminobenzolsulfonacetamide
  as 市販品) — http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/056(1941).pdf

### 7) ウレロン (Uleron) — **検証済み / VERIFIED** (as a Japanese-made equivalent)

* **(a) Exact Japanese names:** The German product is called **ウレロン / ウリロン (Uleron / Uliron,
  = Diseptal A)**. Its **Japanese market names** are, per the 1942 official report:
  **アルバジール、ウリノーゲン、ユーロン、ヂセプタール、ネオアドロン**.
  The corresponding Japanese-made product examined in the 1939 urology paper is **アルバジール**
  (romanised in its own German abstract as **Albasil**).
* **(b) Company:** **山之内製薬 (Yamanouchi Pharmaceutical)** for **アルバジル / Albasil**
  (confirmed twice: Yamanouchi's own 50-year history chapter title **「ゲリゾン」と「アルバジル」の開発**,
  and the academic article attributing Albasil to Yamanouchi).
  Makers of ウリノーゲン / ユーロン / ヂセプタール / ネオアドロン: **查不到**, except
  **ウリノーゲン = 塩野義製薬, 昭和14年 (1939)**, per Shionogi's centenary history.
* **(c) Year on sale in Japan:** **昭和13年 9月 (September 1938)** for **アルバジル**.
  (ゲリゾン preceded it in 昭和12年7月 / July 1937.)
* **Peer-reviewed confirmation of the identification:**
  川島甲子雄・長谷川季夫「『アルバジール』(邦製『ウリロン』) 臨床應用成績」日本泌尿器科學會雑誌

  *"Nachträglich haben wir bei 52 Fällen (hauptsächlich Gonorrhoe) mit **Albasil
  (Uliron-Präparat in Japan)** behandelt."*

### 8) ネオプロントジル (Neoprontosil) — **查不到 / UNVERIFIED**

* **No evidence found that "ネオプロントジル" was used as a trade name in Japan, or that Neoprontosil
  was sold in Japan under that name.** I am explicitly not asserting it.
* What Japanese sources *do* use for the soluble form is **水溶性プロントジル** (and **溶性プロントジル**),
  as distinct from **赤色プロントジル**. The 1942 official report's Japanese-name list for the
  azo-dye group contains **テラポール・ゲリゾン・ルジール・白色アクチヅール・ポンヂール・プロセプチン・
  プレニン** — **ネオプロントジル is not among them**, and it is not among the ウレロン-group names either.
* German forms named in the same report: **ヂセプタールB = 外國名ネオウリロン, 日本名アナビオン**
  (that is *Neo-Uliron*, not Neoprontosil).
* **Sources for the negative finding:** 厚生省東京衛生試験所彙報 第59號 (1942) —
  http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/059(1942).pdf ;
  同 第56號 (1941) — http://www.nihs.go.jp/library/039-077/056(1941).pdf ;
  and repeated Japanese-language web searching (ネオプロントジル + 日本/昭和/發賣/製薬).

### 9) サルファ剤 (generic term usage) — **検証済み / VERIFIED**

* **サルファ剤** is attested as a live Japanese generic term in the 1935–45 period — e.g. the 1941
  Tokyo Takarazuka Theatre programme advertisement is described as an ad for **サルファ剤の『アルバジル錠』**,
  and the 1952 medical literature uses 「ス剤」 as an abbreviation for サルファ剤/スルホンアミド剤.
* The **contemporary technical/official** term was **スルホンアミド剤** (also written スルフオンアミド剤)
  and **スルファミン剤**; the 1942 official report is titled 「スルホンアミド剤の毒性に關する調査報告」,
  and the 1951 industry report uses the phrase 「スルホンアミド剤發展の跡を眺めると Uleron (Domagk 1936),
  Sulfapyridin (Whitby 1938), Albucid (Dohrn 1938) が夫々一轉期を割してゐる」.
* In the **Chinese** literature the group is 磺胺 (huáng'àn); Chinese sources do not supply Japanese
  trade names, and I found no Chinese source that reliably names Japanese サルファ剤 brands.

## 6. Things in the original list that are wrong or misleading (flagged)

1. **アルブシッド ≠ アルバジル.** Albucid's ingredient is **sulfacetamide** and its Japanese names were
   **ネオポレオン / レギオン**. Albasil/アルバジル is Yamanouchi's **Uleron**-type product. Different
   substances, different companies. The question's framing conflates them.
2. **"ALBASIL" is not an Albucid tablet.** The Yamanouchi tablet in the NMTH collection is a
   **Uleron (Diseptal A)-class** product, per the 1939 Japanese journal title and abstract.
3. **ネオプロントジル: no Japanese trade name exists in the sources.** Use 水溶性プロントジル.
4. **スルファダイアジン in Japan is post-war (1948, テラジアジン / 第一製薬).** It does not belong to
   a 1935–45 list.
5. **スルファミン is a pharmacopoeia name, not a trade name.** Wartime Japanese practice was to sell
   under the generic スルファミン with maker-specific secondary names, which is exactly why the
   1941 official report complains about name abuse.
6. **No wartime sulfonamide product could be attributed to 武田, 三共, 星製薬, 持田 or 日本新薬** on the
   evidence available. Absence of evidence here is stated as such, not as proof of absence — those
   company histories (武田二百年史, 三共60年史, 第一製薬九十年史) were not machine-readable in this
   sandbox and remain the obvious next step for a definitive answer.
